## Findings

### Variable RSS is observed, its cause is not identified

The 60-second combined runs each used ten idle OpenCode TUIs and two shell/build panes, completed 55 measured fixture builds, and sampled 60 times at roughly 1 Hz. Run 6's Panelink harness RSS was 13.84 MiB initially, 53.84 MiB at p50, and 60.11 MiB maximum. Run 7 began at 13.22 MiB, rose sharply between the 20- and 30-second samples (49 MiB to 338 MiB), reached 345.19 MiB maximum, and had 240.02 MiB p50. Thus the observed peak differs by 285.08 MiB between runs. OpenCode's aggregate RSS was approximately 1.6–1.8 GiB in both runs. Shell/build process-group sampled maximum was 88.66 MiB in run 6 and 98.17 MiB in run 7.

These values describe the harness process and sampled process groups. They do not describe one deployed server, live Rust heap, allocator-retained pages, or short-lived peaks between samples. The report does not attribute individual allocations, record process child RSS ancestry at sub-second cadence, instrument allocator state, or instrument history queue occupancy. The separate build process group is sampled, but these observations cannot prove whether a transient compiler/build child, terminal traffic processing, checkpoints, clients, or allocator retention accounts for the harness's RSS increase. The current evidence supports “large run-to-run RSS variation”; it does not support a leak or a capture-queue memory diagnosis.

The combined harness hosts six in-process drivers and their clients, and launches ten external OpenCode TUI processes. That is materially different from the deployed server process. The existing resource sampling is a useful workload proxy only.

### Retention overwrite and capture drop are different outcomes

The combined paced shell ended with about 4,300 produced lines and retained exactly 1,024 rows / about 4.997 MB with an explicit gap. Its history writer drained, all targets ended, and no sticky capture failure or diagnostic reason was reported. That gap is consistent with the documented 1,024-row retention bound; it must not be classified as writer loss. The build panes retained 39–40 rows without a gap.

The synthetic actual-store `burst_238` and `shutdown_238` cases report `admission_bytes_full` and `final_payload_dropped_bytes` while the writer's accepted work ultimately reports `drained`. The ordinary burst producers completed 20,000 rows per pane, but only a 728-row suffix per pane was present and a gap was marked. `shutdown_238` did not complete producers. A drained writer means its accepted queue completed; it does not mean every attempted final payload was admitted. This is confirmed capture loss under the deliberately wide burst, not merely retention eviction. The 32 MiB writer byte budget is payload admission accounting, including queued and in-flight batches; it is not a process RSS ceiling.

## Source trace and allocation-lifetime observations

The code graph was queried first in the indexed project `Users-sahillamba-.codex-worktrees-7060-panelink`; current source was then checked at the references below.

- `src/server_pty.rs:644-724`, `PtyEffects::capture_history_sync`: reads rows after the prior cursor, collects borrowed engine rows into a `Vec`, encodes each row into a second `Vec<Vec<u8>>`, then appends to the store. On engine `Gap`/`Reset`, it starts from the newest 1,024-row suffix. This is live scrollback capture, separate from the asynchronous checkpoint/history writer path.
- `src/history_capture.rs:413-517`, `HistoryCaptureCoordinator::observe`: merges/coalesces at most the latest normal and alternate checkpoint per target. It clones checkpoint bytes to create a candidate batch, submits rows and screens to the writer, and on `Full` deliberately does not retain row payloads outside the writer quota. A later observation can retry from the advanced source; gaps are tracked explicitly. Screen checkpoint records can remain deferred while rows are not retained after a full admission result.
- `src/history_capture.rs:149-264`, `TargetCapture`: each capture target holds bounded receipt/commit metadata and two deferred checkpoint slots. Screen payload generation clones the deferred checkpoint bytes; identities are separately collected for commit bookkeeping.
- `src/history_writer.rs:26-38, 781-809`: writer admission is bounded at 16 accepted batches and 32 MiB encoded payload, including the in-flight batch; each batch allows up to 1,024 rows plus two checkpoint payloads. A full admission is a regular outcome with a diagnostic reason, not proof the worker failed.
- `src/history_writer.rs:46-158`: `HistoryBatch::new` validates the owned payload and converts row/screen vectors into boxed slices. During conversion the original row allocations and newly boxed allocations may briefly overlap; encoded byte accounting measures owned payload bytes, not allocator overhead or transient duplicate-capacity bytes. This is a plausible burst-time RSS contributor, not evidence explaining run 7's sustained-sized RSS step.
- `src/history_capture.rs:894-919`, `final_payload_lost`: when the required final payload cannot be submitted, records loss and end metadata, sets a gap, discards deferred screens, and makes loss observable. This preserves truthful failure reporting but confirms that final payload bytes can be lost under pressure.
- `src/server_pty.rs:1446-1496`, `live_scrollback_text`: allocates an output `String` with the caller's byte bound and walks only the requested row tail. This is a bounded read path; it is not evidence of the combined harness's RSS jump.

### Ranked hypotheses and falsifiers

1. **Harness-side terminal processing or snapshots transiently allocate a large working set.** The panelink process contains six drivers and clients; the observed jump is in that aggregate process. Falsify by sampling per-thread/allocator allocation sites and per-driver counters while repeating the exact load; compare a server-only process under equivalent PTY output.
2. **Repeated record/screen construction temporarily duplicates encoded payload memory.** Source confirms temporary vectors and boxed payload conversion, but workloads that only emit a few thousand paced lines should be small relative to a 285 MiB between-run delta. Falsify by recording per-batch live/peak bytes, allocation counts, and history admission occupancy; an unchanged run-7-sized jump with no large batches rules this down.
3. **Allocator or OS page retention keeps RSS high after transient allocations end.** RSS alone cannot distinguish live objects from retained pages. Falsify with allocator live/retained statistics plus post-workload quiescence and a fresh-process control; do not use RSS decline or non-decline alone to infer a leak.
4. **Build activity or child-process accounting contaminates attribution.** Run 7's separate build-process-group sampled maximum was under 100 MiB, below the harness's 345 MiB peak, but 1 Hz samples and process-group aggregation do not rule out short-lived peaks or indirect workload interactions. Falsify by recording child process ancestry and sub-second RSS, then repeat with builds disabled and with builds isolated from the measured process tree.
5. **History queue pressure contributes to RSS in the combined workload.** Current combined runs report no history failure, and queue occupancy is not instrumented in their report. Falsify by exposing bounded counters for submitted/rejected/in-flight payload bytes and candidate batch bytes in a diagnostic build, then correlate them with process/allocator samples. Do not change production limits to test this.

## Proposed performance acceptance criteria (not approved)

Use the same pinned Apple Silicon/macOS environment and report both deployed-server and harness measurements. The first two historical combined runs are a starting comparison, not a statistically reliable baseline. Retain raw samples and use nearest-rank percentiles. Run three serial repetitions of at least five measured minutes after a two-minute warm-up; keep ten idle OpenCode sessions, two shell/build panes, 60–72 acknowledged probes per minute, paced output, and builds enabled. Also run a three-repetition, five-minute no-build control and an isolated paced-history workload. Do not submit prompts or model calls. Record exact launcher and child identity as the existing workload did.

RSS criteria are not live-heap limits and do not promise reboot durability. Reboot/layout restoration remains a distinct acceptance gate.
